## Full brief (E-E-A-T)

### Experience — how the film is actually built

A 52-second vertical explainer is assembled as **data**, not as a timeline edit: one scene accepts any narration in any language, and every beat re-times itself to that voice.

- The narrator’s take is never time-stretched and never cut inside a line.
- The client’s music track plays as delivered from 0:00.
- Headroom for the voice is carved with a **static EQ**, never with volume automation.

### Expertise — craft rules

**Safe area.** All graphics stay inside the Shorts/Reels safe frame (nothing above 250 px, below 1500 px, or right of 950 px). The subtitle band is masked clean so no stroke crosses the text; the title plaque never moves.

**Graphic types.**

- Base markup is printed and traced in as a wave (outline → fill).
- Coloured chalk strokes are drawn along their own path, with grain and glow.
- Handwritten notes are written letter by letter along the glyph centreline (Latin and Cyrillic, accents included).
- Archive photos open full-frame, then fly onto the board as pinned prints.

**Timing.**

- Every action is keyed to a word of the narration through word-level timestamps. A new take or a new language moves the whole film with it.
- The cut is exactly one loop of the music, so replays are seamless and the music’s accent lands on the visual peak.

### Trust — what is forbidden, how we check

**Forbidden.**

- Fades or wipes in place of drawing.
- More than one main object and two details per frame (one density burst of up to 2 s allowed per film).
- Labels too small to read on a phone.
- Decoration that carries no meaning.

**Checks before every render.** A script validates the plan for every voice: beat order, reading time for each note, subtitle size, and fit inside the music loop. A component sheet shows every glyph, stroke and figure at full size before anything ships.

### Authoritativeness — what this category claims

This is a **voice-locked** pipeline: the animation is a function of the narration timestamps and the music loop length. Changing the language or the take updates the motion; the rules above do not change.
