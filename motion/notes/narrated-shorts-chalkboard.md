## Home blurb (used on `index.html`)

A vertical Short built as data, not a timeline edit: one scene, every action keyed to a spoken word so a new take re-times the whole film. Base markup is printed and traced in as a wave (outline→fill). Coloured chalk strokes draw along their own path, with grain and glow. Handwritten notes write letter by letter along the glyph centreline — Latin and Cyrillic, accents included. Archive photos open full-frame, then fly onto the board as pinned prints. Everything stays inside the Shorts safe area; the subtitle band is masked clean so no stroke crosses the text; the title plaque never moves. No fades or wipes in place of drawing; at most one main object and two details per frame (one density burst of up to 2 s allowed). Labels must read on a phone; decoration that carries no meaning is out. Before every render, a script checks beat order, reading time for each note, subtitle size, and fit inside the loop; a component sheet shows every glyph, stroke and figure at full size before anything ships.

## Full brief (E-E-A-T)

### Experience — how the film is built

A vertical Short assembled as **data**, not a timeline edit: one scene, every action keyed to a spoken word. A new take re-times the whole film.

### Expertise — craft rules

**Safe area.** All graphics stay inside the Shorts/Reels safe frame. The subtitle band is masked clean so no stroke crosses the text; the title plaque never moves.

**Graphic types.**

- Base markup is printed and traced in as a wave (outline → fill).
- Coloured chalk strokes are drawn along their own path, with grain and glow.
- Handwritten notes are written letter by letter along the glyph centreline (Latin and Cyrillic, accents included).
- Archive photos open full-frame, then fly onto the board as pinned prints.

### Trust — what is forbidden, how we check

**Forbidden.**

- Fades or wipes in place of drawing.
- More than one main object and two details per frame (one density burst of up to 2 s allowed per film).
- Labels too small to read on a phone.
- Decoration that carries no meaning.

**Checks before every render.** A script validates beat order, reading time for each note, subtitle size, and fit inside the loop. A component sheet shows every glyph, stroke and figure at full size before anything ships.
